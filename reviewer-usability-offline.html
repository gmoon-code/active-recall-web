Active Recall Web · Fictional reviewer-usability exercise · Free and offline

Tell us what is confusing about evaluating an explanation.

This optional exercise contains only fictional scientific explanations. It does not grade your expertise, measure learning or require a name or account. You can stop at any time without producing a file. First make your own judgments, then answer or skip short usability questions, and only afterward see discussion guidance in the interface. Your results remain on this device until you choose to download and share a file.

1. Open your invitation

2. Make your own judgments on fictional explanations

For each criterion, select Included, Not demonstrated, Uncertain or Not assessed. For Included, copy an exact excerpt from the fictional response. If you choose Not demonstrated or Uncertain, briefly explain your reasoning. If a criterion seems difficult to use, flag what makes it unclear. You may leave a criterion Not assessed.

3. Optional feedback about the instructions

For each statement, choose how clear that part of the task felt. A response of 1 means strongly disagree and 5 means strongly agree. You can choose Skip for any question. These ratings describe your experience, not your science expertise.

Avoid names, school details, identifiable student records or other sensitive information. The coordinator can read any free-text comment if you share your response file, but their coded report intentionally omits your words.

4. Discuss rubric clarity afterward

Your response has been downloaded and is fixed in this page session. These are interpretive prompts, not an answer key or a certificate of reviewer accuracy. You may share the downloaded JSON with the trusted coordinator if you choose.

This exercise cannot verify your identity, whether you had seen the prompts elsewhere, or whether other people participated. Criteria should only be revised for future assessments, never applied backward to previous study records.
